The constraint

Firms and in-house teams face a specific version of the problem: the duty of confidentiality and the duty of technology competence point in opposite directions when the only available tooling requires uploading client material to a third party.

Add the defensibility standard in e-discovery, and "the model produced this" is not a process you can put in front of a court.

Every open task on a matter is a risk — an unanswered client, a missed deadline.

ChatGPT and Copilot are not a matter workflow

ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot help people draft and research in a separate window.

A lawyer pastes confidential text into a chat to get a draft; the chain of custody of that text is unclear, and a later write back into the document system, the file, or the mail goes out with no approval on the write itself.

The chat was useful. It was not a controlled close.

Whether Copilot is safer than a consumer ChatGPT account depends on the tenant controls and on the firm's own policy. Neither is a blank check to paste matter content.

The alternative is to watch the work in the systems the firm already uses, show the proposed write, and execute only after a lawyer approves it, in the deployment setup the firm's policy calls for.

What was reviewed, proposed, approved and executed.

  • Human approval gates. Flow prepares the update and a lawyer approves it. Judgment calls surface for a lawyer rather than executing silently.
  • Deterministic execution. Workflow steps run under a strict runtime rather than free-form model output.
  • A record you can defend. What was reviewed, what was proposed, who approved it and what was executed. The scope of the audit log is set with your security team in the engagement, so the process can be reconstructed when it is challenged.
  • Does not replace your document system. Flow works across the systems the firm already uses, after a person approves the write. It does not become the system of record.
