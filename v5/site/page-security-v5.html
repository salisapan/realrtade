Deployment Models

The most important security decision is where processing happens. The office chooses between three setups:

Flow's Dedicated Secure CloudFlow's own dedicated cloud environment. All sensitive data — personally identifiable and confidential fields — is masked before any processing.
Flow-EdgeFor computers with suitable hardware. Data is stored only on the employee's own computer and never leaves it.
Flow-On-PremA dedicated server inside the office's internal network. Data never goes to an external cloud.

Data Masking

In Flow's dedicated secure cloud, sensitive fields are identified and replaced with structure-preserving placeholders before processing.

The model operates on the masked representation, with the mapping back to real values staying under your control, so a prompt, a log, or a model provider does not receive the underlying identifier.

Masking is a mitigation, not a substitute for architecture.

Where the requirement is "this data must not leave the building," the right setups are Flow-Edge or Flow-On-Prem. We will say that directly rather than offer a cloud deployment with extra controls as if it were the same thing.

Zero-Retention Processing

Flow is designed so that content processed through the engine is not retained for model training and is not persisted beyond the life of the task that required it.

"Zero-retention" is a claim worth verifying with any vendor — including us.

We wrote a guide on how to verify a vendor's zero-retention claim, and we expect you to apply it to Flow.

Audit Logging & Provenance

Audit trail. For regulated deployments, the scope of the audit log — what was observed, what was proposed, who approved it, and what was executed — is agreed with your security team in the engagement.

The purpose is defensibility: when a regulator, auditor, or opposing counsel asks why a system did something eighteen months ago, the answer must be reconstructible from records rather than from memory.

Related reading: Auditability by Design and Tracing a workflow decision back to its source.

Human Approval Gates

High-risk actions require explicit human approval before execution. The engine surfaces the proposed action with its supporting context; a person accepts or rejects it. Autonomy is bounded by policy you define, not by the model's confidence in itself.

Deterministic Execution

Workflow execution runs under a strict runtime rather than free-form model output.

The distinction matters for regulated use: a system that "usually" files the document correctly is not deployable in an environment where the failure mode is a reportable incident.

Transport & Application Security

Transport.
All traffic to theflow-ai.com and to Flow services is served over HTTPS with HSTS enforced.
Browser hardening.
The site is served with a strict Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options: DENY, X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, and a restrictive Permissions-Policy.
Waitlist data.
Signups are confirmed by email double opt-in, so an address cannot be added by someone who does not control it.

Threat Model

We treat prompt injection and data exfiltration through model outputs as first-class threats rather than edge cases, because an automation engine with real system access has a materially larger blast radius than a chat interface.

Our analysis is public: Prompt Injection and Data Exfiltration: Threat Modeling AI Workflow Automation.

Compliance Posture

The architecture above is designed to support deployment under HIPAA, GDPR, SOC 2, and sector-specific frameworks including 21 CFR Part 11, NERC CIP, CJIS, and FedRAMP-aligned government requirements.
