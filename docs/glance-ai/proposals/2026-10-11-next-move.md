## בעברית, בקצרה

כש־Glance שלח שלוש תזכורות ואין תשובה, היום הוא פשוט שולח שוב את התזכורת האחרונה, כל יומיים־שלושה, בלי סוף. ההצעה: Glance מציע מהלך אחד אחר, רק על סמך מה שהוא כבר יודע:
- **«את התשובות מדנה קיבלת בוואטסאפ. לשאול שם?»**: האפליקציה שבה האדם הזה באמת עונה לך.
- **«לבקש מיוסי לעזור?»**: עמית אחד על השרשור, מאותו ארגון, שכבר ענה לך בעבר.
- **«עדיין צריך את זה?»**: «עזוב» הוא שחרור מכוון, וזה סוגר את הלולאה ביושר.

שום מהלך לא נשלח ולא נסגר לבד. בסימולציה: לולאות פתוחות אחרי 90 יום ירדו מ־23.4% ל־7.0%, ופחות לולאות נשארו בלי תשובה (9.5%→6.9%). זו סימולציה, לא משתמשים אמיתיים.

## The gap (evidence)

1. **Today a stuck loop has no next move.** `core/follow-up.js` caps the nudge at level 3 (`MAX_NUDGE_LEVEL = 3`, `nextNudgeLevel` returns 3 forever). `recordNudge` moves the chase day out and the same last, direct nudge comes back. Nothing else happens until they answer. In the simulation below, a person who will never answer gets **31.8 nudges per loop** under today's rule, and **23.4%** of all loops are still open at day 90.
2. **A plain loop cannot be released.** The locked identity says a loop is closed "only on real completion or a deliberate release". In the general follow loop, `released` exists only for items (`decideItem`, `CLIENT_REQUESTS.items`). A plain loop ends only on an answer, or as `stopped` via Undo (`src/follow.js`). Without a release, the list fills with loops that will never close, and "Waiting or tracking alone is not success" (`CLAUDE.md` standing constraints) is exactly what the person sees.
3. **The evidence Glance needs is already stored.** Closed loops carry `channel`, `personKey` (one person across apps, `core/identity-graph.js`), `createdAt` and `resolvedAt`. `core/person-model.js` already models each person's pace. Nothing uses that evidence to change *how* Glance chases.
4. **The owner's edge, 2026-10-10:** carry until closed. Glance stays on a loop until it really closes and makes it happen (`docs/open-tasks.md` row 68). Holistic close map E7: "Tracking is not the close" (score 144, partial).

This does not duplicate M1–M8 (the model's Understanding/Plan), the breadth builder (new catalog rows), or Dima's queue (rows 51–53). It is a decision inside the follow loop.

## The user moment

Dana has not answered three reminders about the signed lease. On the next chase day, Glance's card does not show a fourth reminder. It says: **«Dana usually answers you on WhatsApp. Ask there?»** It also has the line ready: "Hi Dana, I sent you a message on Gmail about the signed lease and haven't heard back. Could you take a look?" The person opens the chat and sends it themselves. If nothing points anywhere else and it has been weeks, the card asks: **«No answer from Dana after 3 reminders and 35 days. Still need this?»** The choices are *Keep chasing* and *Let it go*.

## How it closes a loop (ProofOfClose)

- A move **never closes** the loop (`closes: false`, `needsClick: true`). The loop still closes only on their real answer, in any app, read back. A WhatsApp or other-app answer reaches the loop through `core/cross-channel.js` `judge`, with `fetchedBack` on that message.
- **Let it go** is the person's deliberate release: `releasePatch` sets `status: 'resolved'`, `closedAs: 'released'`, `resolvedBy: 'person'`. It is never counted as Glance's own close or a proved close (`weekly_closes`). It is also not a real close for the person model, so it does not teach the model that this person answered.
- Money is **never** released by a question. A payment loop can get *channel* or *colleague*, never *release*.
- Glance never sends. The channel and colleague moves are prepared text; the person sends it in that app themselves. (`Mail.Send`, row 53, would later be the preview for the mail case.)

## What the prototype decides (variant B)

Only when the switch is on, the loop is theirs (not your promise, not a clock, not "yours", not "promised"), has no items, and has had **three nudges**, and only on a chase day:

| Move | Evidence it needs | Silence when |
|---|---|---|
| channel | ≥ 2 real closes with the same person (same `personKey`) in another app the surface can prepare, and no or clearly slower closes here | Another key (not proven the same person), 1 close, they answer here just as well |
| colleague | Exactly one person on this thread, same organisation (not free mail), ≥ 2 real closes with you | Two candidates, another organisation, gmail.com, 1 close |
| release (question) | ≥ 3 real closes: silence ≥ 3× the longest they ever took and ≥ 10 business days. Otherwise ≥ 20 business days | A slow person still inside their own pace; any payment loop |

Each move type is proposed at most once per loop. A dismissed one is never proposed again on that loop. With no move, B does exactly what A does.

I first tried the person model's conditional probability (`pWithin`) as the release trigger. It did not separate people: the log-normal tail keeps P(answer in 10 more days) around 0.45–0.69, even for someone who always answers within a day. So the trigger is the plain rule above, which a person can also read and check.

## A/B

- **A (today):** three nudges, then the last nudge repeated on every chase day.
- **B (new):** the same three nudges, then `nextMove`. With no move, the same as A.
- **Data set:** `scripts/carry-sim.cjs`, a **seeded simulation** (seed 20261011). It has 320 synthetic people, 632 evaluation loops (306 Hebrew, 326 English) and 1,606 history loops, with 90 days of follow-up. It drives the real `core/` functions. A and B get the same random draw for the same message. People's behaviour (five types: answers mail, answers only chat, slow, gone, needs a colleague) and the accept rates are **assumptions** written at the top of the script. There is no real loop-lifecycle data (no installs, row 64), and `glance-ai/eval-data/` has single mails, not loop histories, so it cannot score this.
- **Metrics:**
  - wrong Do It: here, an early release, meaning the person let go of a loop that would still have been answered
  - missed close: a reachable person never answered
  - loops open at day 90
  - nudges per loop
  - moves with no answer

## Risks and what stays locked

- **Wrong person.** A colleague move needs exactly one candidate on the thread, at the same organisation, with real closes. A channel move needs the same `personKey`, a hard-key merge in the identity graph; a shared name is never enough.
- **Pressure.** A move is proposed once, and a "no" is remembered. Hebrew copy is gender-neutral («את התשובות מדנה קיבלת בוואטסאפ», «אפשר להציץ», «אפשר לעזור לי», «עדיין צריך את זה?»).
- **Early release** is the one harm. It is the person's choice on a question, never automatic, never for money. It is measured above: 1/632, worst case 4/632.
- Locked:
  - no send without the person
  - silence when there is no evidence
  - nothing leaves the device (the decision reads only stored loop metadata, no mail text)
  - switch off ≡ today (pinned in the corpus and the simulation)
- **UI:** the card text and the two buttons wait for the owner's approval of a mock. There is no new page or settings screen.

## Experiment built (2026-10-11, finish run)

### What was finished

Edge cases:
- **A promised day that passed with no answer.** The loop gets a move again. A promised day that is today, or still ahead, still waits.
- **Two mail apps count as one channel.** Gmail and Outlook are the same way to reach the person, so there is no "ask on Outlook" move from a Gmail loop. Mail is still proposed from a WhatsApp loop: «את התשובות מדנה קיבלת באאוטלוק».
- **A colleague known under another address.** A colleague is counted through the identity key (`opts.keyOf`), not only by the exact address.
- **A "no" to a colleague is remembered.** If the person declined asking Yossi about Dana on any loop, Glance does not suggest him again for Dana for 90 days. `moveDismissPatch` now stores who was declined.
- **Missing name or subject.** The question still reads correctly with no name or no subject.

All of these are pinned in Hebrew and English: `carry-next-move-corpus`, 63 checks.

### Real-user A/B layer (dormant)

The layer is in `core/pmf-metrics.js`, the existing metrics module:
- **`assignVariant(installId, 'carry-next-move', { enabled })`** gives a deterministic arm from the install id alone. With the switch off, a missing id or an unknown experiment it returns `null`, and the caller behaves as today.
  - The arms are balanced: over 4,000 installs, A gets between 45% and 55%.
- **`foldAbEvent`** keeps per-arm counters of a fixed list of event names. Free text, an unknown event or an unknown arm is never stored, and the stored state holds only numbers.
- **`abSnapshot`** gives per-arm rates with their n. A rate on zero is null, not 0. The early-release signal is `reopened_after_release`.
- **Nothing is sent and nothing is stored** while `CARRY.abLive` is off. Turning it on is the owner's decision, with the privacy page changed in the same commit.

### Recommendation

**Keep, and run it with real users when the owner decides.**

In simulation, B roughly halves the nudges per loop. It cuts the loops that sit open for 90 days by about two thirds, and gets more real answers from people who answer elsewhere or through a colleague.

The one harm is an early release. It stays rare (0.2%, worst case 0.6%), and the person always makes it.

What these numbers do not show:
- How real people answer.
- How often a person accepts a move.
