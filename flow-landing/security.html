Security & Architecture

Last updated: September 2, 2026

Flow is built for organizations that cannot send raw sensitive data to a third-party model — legal, healthcare, finance, insurance, and government. This page describes how the system is designed, so your security team can evaluate it without a call.

1. Two Deployment Models

The most important security decision is where processing happens. Flow supports two models, and you choose per deployment:

Masked Secure Cloud

Processing runs in Flow's managed environment. Personally identifiable and confidential fields are detected and masked before any data leaves your environment for processing.

Flow-Edge (Fully Local)

The entire engine runs inside your own infrastructure. Data never leaves your network boundary. This is the model for environments where policy or regulation prohibits external processing outright.

If your obligations make cloud processing untenable — export-controlled manufacturing data, NERC CIP-scoped operational systems, classified or CUI-adjacent government work — Flow-Edge is the deployment answer, not a configuration flag on a cloud product.

2. Data Masking

In the cloud deployment model, sensitive fields are identified and replaced with structure-preserving placeholders before processing. The model operates on the masked representation; the mapping back to real values stays under your control. This means a prompt, a log, or a model provider never receives the underlying identifier.

Masking is a mitigation, not a substitute for architecture. Where the requirement is "this data must not leave the building," the correct answer is Flow-Edge. We will tell you that directly rather than sell you a cloud deployment with extra controls.

3. Zero-Retention Processing

Flow is designed so that content processed through the engine is not retained for model training and is not persisted beyond the life of the task that required it. "Zero-retention" is a claim worth verifying with any vendor — including us. We wrote a guide on how to verify a vendor's zero-retention claim, and we expect you to apply it to Flow.

4. Audit Logging & Provenance

Every action the engine takes is recorded in an append-only log: what was observed, what was proposed, who approved it, and what was executed. The purpose is defensibility — when a regulator, auditor, or opposing counsel asks why a system did something eighteen months ago, the answer must be reconstructible from records rather than from memory.

Related reading: Auditability by Design and Tracing a workflow decision back to its source.

5. Human Approval Gates

High-risk actions require explicit human approval before execution. The engine surfaces the proposed action with its supporting context; a person accepts or rejects it. Autonomy is bounded by policy you define, not by the model's confidence in itself.

6. Deterministic Execution

Workflow execution runs under a strict runtime rather than free-form model output. The distinction matters for regulated use: a system that "usually" files the document correctly is not deployable in an environment where the failure mode is a reportable incident.

7. Transport & Application Security

  • Transport. All traffic to theflow-ai.com and to Flow services is served over HTTPS with HSTS enforced.
  • Browser hardening. The site is served with a strict Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options: DENY, X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, and a restrictive Permissions-Policy.
  • Waitlist data. Signups are confirmed by email double opt-in, so an address cannot be added by someone who does not control it.

8. Threat Model

We treat prompt injection and data exfiltration through model outputs as first-class threats rather than edge cases, because an automation engine with real system access has a materially larger blast radius than a chat interface. Our analysis is public: Prompt Injection and Data Exfiltration: Threat Modeling AI Workflow Automation.

9. Compliance Posture

Flow is currently in pre-release. The architecture above is designed to support deployment under HIPAA, GDPR, SOC 2, and sector-specific frameworks including 21 CFR Part 11, NERC CIP, CJIS, and FedRAMP-aligned government requirements.

If you are running a formal vendor assessment, contact us at hello@theflow-ai.com for the current status of individual certifications and audit reports, along with the deployment model that fits your control environment. We would rather give you an accurate answer in writing than have you infer a certification status from a badge.

10. Reporting a Vulnerability

If you believe you have found a security issue in Flow or in this site, email hello@theflow-ai.com with the subject line "Security vulnerability report". Please include enough detail to reproduce the issue. We will acknowledge your report and will not pursue action against good-faith research that avoids privacy violations, service disruption, and data destruction.
