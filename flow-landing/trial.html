01

What it is, and deliberately isn't

It is

  • Passive — silent until something is actually decided. Most days it says nothing.
  • Zero-prompt — you never type anything, at setup or at runtime. There is no box to write in.
  • Cross-platform — it reaches out of your inbox into a system that isn't Google's, which is the whole point.
  • Local — the decision to speak is made on your device. No email text is sent anywhere to reach it.
  • Reversible — every write returns an Undo. Nothing it does is one-way.

It isn't

  • A chat assistant. There is nothing to ask it.
  • A rule builder. There is no "when X happens, do Y" screen. That is Zapier, and it is exactly the configuration burden Flow exists to remove.
  • A nagging notifier. If nothing happens for two days, that is the product working correctly.
  • A mailbox crawler. It only ever reads the one message open in front of you.
02

How it works

Four moments. Three of them are silence.

1

Connect, in a minute

Paste one credential for where Flow may write, and answer one question about the kind of work you do. That is the entire setup. No field mapping, no rules, no trigger screen.

2

It stays quiet

Most days nothing happens. A tool that talks when it has nothing to say gets uninstalled by day two, so silence is the default state and the bar to break it is deliberately high.

3

The moment

An email decides something — a figure agreed, a date committed to, a deal lost. Flow reads the figure, the date and the sentence that carried it, and puts one small button beside that message. Not a popup. Not a notification.

4

Click, or ignore

Click and the record is written, with View and Undo beside it. Ignore it and it disappears for good. Either way Flow gets slightly more or less willing to speak next time — the one thing it learns, and you never set a number for it.

The trade this build makes, stated plainly: the scorer that decides whether to speak is a transparent weighting of named signals, not a language model. That is what lets it run entirely on your device and explain itself — and it is also why a model would catch phrasings this one misses. We took explainable and local over slightly better recall. 04

What it does with your data

This reads the email in front of you, so it owes you a precise answer rather than a generic privacy paragraph.

What it touches

  • The single message open in Gmail's reading pane — not your inbox, not older messages, not attachments
  • When you click: the destination you connected, to create one new record there
  • Locally, on your machine: which system you connected, the kind of work you chose, and a log of what Flow proposed

What it never does

  • It never sends your email anywhere to decide. The judgment is computed on your device, in the page.
  • No ad targeting, no reselling, no training a model on your mail
  • Never edits or deletes anything you already had — it only ever adds one record, and hands you the Undo
  • No crawling your mailbox in the background

The only network calls this extension makes are to the system you chose to connect, and only at the moment you click. Your credential, the kind of work you picked, and the activity log live in local browser storage — uninstalling deletes all of it. Records already written stay where they were written, because that write really happened; each one has an Undo next to it for as long as the log keeps it. 05

Questions worth asking first

Does clicking Do It really change anything?

Yes. It creates a real record in the system you connected — a page in your Notion database, or a Note on the matching HubSpot Contact — with the amount, the date, the sender, the quoted sentence and a link back to the email. Open it afterwards and it is there. Every write comes back with an Undo, and undoing it removes the record.

How does it decide when to speak?

It scores the open message against weighted signals: a currency figure, a commitment verb, a dated obligation, a direct request, a stated loss — set against negatives like an automated sender or mailing-list boilerplate. A figure with nothing decided around it is pushed back down, which is what keeps price lists quiet. It speaks only above a threshold, and that threshold moves as you click and dismiss. All of it runs on your device.

What do I have to configure?

Two things, once. Where Flow may write, and what kind of work you do. There is no trigger screen, no field mapping and no rule syntax — that absence is the product, not a missing feature.

Is my email sent to a server?

Not to decide. The scoring happens in the page, on your machine. The only outbound call is to the system you connected, and only at the moment you click Do It.

What if the Notion database has different columns?

It adapts. Flow fills the title, then matches the rest by name and type — a number column called Amount, a date column called Due, an email column, a URL column. Anything it cannot map still goes into the page body, so nothing it extracted is quietly dropped.

What happens if I ignore a suggestion?

It disappears and Flow never asks about that message again. Both clicking and dismissing quietly retune how confident it needs to be before speaking next time; the popup shows you where that has landed.

Why isn't it on the Chrome Web Store?

Store submission needs a completed data-use disclosure, and that is in progress. Until then it installs through Chrome's own Load unpacked flow, which takes about a minute and is what the install steps below walk through.

Can I remove my data?

Uninstalling the extension deletes everything it stored on your machine. To remove your early-access email from our list, contact hello@theflow-ai.com.
