AI Automation for Healthcare: PHI, Approval Gates, and Where Processing Runs

AI automation under HIPAA with human approval gates for clinical and administrative workflows, and the three deployment setups a healthcare office can choose between.

Flow Research · 4 min read

Healthcare organizations are not short of automatable work: prior authorization packets, referral intake, claims correspondence, chart summarization for administrative review, cross-state licensure checks in telehealth. What they are short of is a way to automate any of it without routing protected health information through a system whose retention and subprocessor behavior cannot be fully accounted for in a BAA.

The constraint

PHI does not get to leave the boundary because a vendor says it is convenient. Where processing runs is therefore the first decision a healthcare office makes, and Flow lets the office choose between three setups.

How Flow addresses it

  • Flow's dedicated secure cloud. All sensitive data is masked before any processing.
  • Flow-Edge. For computers with suitable hardware: data is stored only on the employee's own computer and never leaves it.
  • Flow-Onprem. A dedicated server inside the office's internal network. Data never goes to an external cloud.

Across all three, high-risk actions wait for a person. Flow prepares the update, shows what will be written and where, and the person approves it before anything is written. Clinical and billing consequences do not get automated away silently. For regulated deployments, the audit-log scope — what was observed, what was proposed, who approved it and what was executed — is set with your security team in the engagement, so an OCR inquiry or an internal investigation has a record to work from.
