AI Workflow vs RPA: When You Need Human Approval Before Every Write

When classic RPA breaks on a UI change, and when an AI workflow still must wait for a human before writing to a system of record.

Flow Research · 4 min read

RPA is a scripted bot on a predictable screen or API. An AI workflow reads intent in messy documents and events, then runs a deterministic step. In regulated work, neither should write to a system of record until a person has approved that write.

RPA

A recorded path: this control, this selector, this field. Fast when the screen never moves. Fragile when it does. Little judgment on an unstructured email or a PDF that is not the form the bot was taught.

AI workflow

Intent and judgment on the messy input, then one determined execution step. Still not a license to write. The sensitive step waits for a person.

Why RPA breaks

RPA stores the path, not the point. When a button moves, the selector misses and the bot stops, or it clicks the wrong control. Unstructured mail and PDFs fail the same way: the bot has no reading of what the document is asking, only a map of where it expected the pixels to be.

Where an AI workflow fits

The fit is friction across files, core systems, and the apps a team already uses. Watch the work, propose one execution, wait. Email can be one of those surfaces. It is not the product.

Approval before the write

Intent can be model-assisted. The write should not be a series of guessed clicks. Execution is a determined step: an API call, or an action on the accessibility tree, after a person approves the sensitive ones. The approval shows the proposed action and the destination. It is not a checkbox after the record has already changed. Where a step is designed to be reversible, the write can be undone. That reverse belongs to the step. It is not a claim that every system of record supports undo.

Hybrid, not a replacement

Keep RPA where the path is stable and the volume is high. Use a Flow-class layer where the input is a document or an event that needs judgment, and the close crosses more than one app. You do not retire every bot to add approval before a write.

Glance is a different product

Glance closes a clear personal intent from Gmail with one Do It, and it stays quiet when it is not sure. It is not a trial of Flow, and it is not how a firm samples this deployment.

Common questions

Do we need to replace all RPA bots with AI agents?

No. Keep RPA on stable, high-volume paths. Add an AI workflow where the input is unstructured and a person must approve the write. The practical pattern is hybrid.

Why do RPA bots break when the UI changes?

They depend on selectors and recorded clicks. A layout change breaks the path. An AI workflow is aimed at intent, then a deterministic adapter (an API or an accessibility-tree action), so the judgment is not stored as a brittle click script.

Does Flow auto-write to client systems?

No. Sensitive writes wait for a human approval of the specific action and the destination. Flow does not treat a model guess as permission to change a system of record.
