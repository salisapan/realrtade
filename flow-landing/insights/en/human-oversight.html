Human oversight

What real human oversight means

Many AI products say a human stays in the loop. The useful question is narrower: can that person see enough, in time, to stop the action, change it or undo it?

Oct 28, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Oversight works only when the reviewer sees the source, the proposal and what was checked.
  • The reviewer needs real options: approve, edit, reject, stop and reverse.
  • Approval belongs before the action, not in a report afterwards.

A name on a log is not oversight

A person who signs off on a batch of results they cannot inspect is not overseeing anything. They are adding a signature. Real oversight means the reviewer understands what the system is about to do and has the authority and the means to say no.

What the law asks for

The EU AI Act sets this out for high-risk systems. Article 14 says the people assigned to oversight should be able to understand the system's capacities and limits, stay aware of the tendency to over-rely on its output (automation bias), decide not to use the output, override or reverse it, and interrupt the system with a stop button or a similar procedure. Those are concrete abilities, not a policy statement.

What it looks like in daily work

  • The reviewer sees the original request, the proposed action and the data it relies on.
  • Approve, edit and reject are equally easy. Rejecting should not take more effort than approving.
  • An action can be paused or stopped, and a completed step can be reversed.
  • Every decision is recorded: who approved what, when and in which system.

How Flow handles it
