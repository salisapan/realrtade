Generative AI in e-discovery

Document review is one of the heaviest costs in litigation, and generative AI can help. What it cannot do is take over the lawyer's responsibility for what is produced and certified.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Discovery must be proportional to the needs of the case.
  • An attorney signs and certifies disclosures and discovery responses.
  • Document the process so it can be explained if challenged.

The rules that frame it

In US federal courts, Rule 26(b)(1) of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure limits discovery to matter relevant to a claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case. Rule 26(g) requires every disclosure and discovery response to be signed by an attorney of record, and the signature carries a certification. Using AI does not change who signs.

Where AI helps

  • First-pass sorting of large document sets by relevance.
  • Finding likely privileged documents for lawyer review.
  • Summarising documents and building chronologies.

Making it defensible

  • Test the approach on a sample that lawyers have reviewed, and record the results.
  • Keep lawyers on privilege decisions and on anything produced.
  • Record the process: which documents, which instructions, which checks.
  • Protect client and third-party data while it is processed.

Confidentiality

Discovery sets contain other people's confidential material as well as the client's. Where it is processed matters as much as how well.

In Flow
