AI in customs and trade

Importers and brokers deal with invoices, packing lists, classifications and partner checks, all under strict rules. Automation can do much of the preparation. A person still has to stand behind every filing.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • CTPAT is a voluntary supply chain security partnership with its own expectations.
  • Sanctions screening against OFAC lists needs a human decision on every possible match.
  • Data accuracy in customs filings is the filer's responsibility.

Supply chain security

CTPAT, run by US Customs and Border Protection, is a voluntary partnership in which companies commit to supply chain security practices. Participation brings benefits, and it also brings expectations about how partners are vetted and how documents are handled.

Sanctions screening

Before doing business with a party, companies check it against the Treasury's OFAC sanctions lists, including the Specially Designated Nationals list. Names are messy: transliteration, abbreviations and common names produce possible matches. An AI tool can find and rank them. Clearing or escalating a match is a human decision, and it should be recorded.

Accuracy is on you

Values, origins and classifications in a customs filing are the filer's statements. A tool that fills a form from an invoice speeds up the work. It does not move the responsibility.

A sensible flow

  • The tool extracts data from invoices and packing lists and prepares the entry.
  • Discrepancies between documents are flagged.
  • Screening results are shown with the reason for each possible match.
  • A person reviews, decides and approves the filing.
