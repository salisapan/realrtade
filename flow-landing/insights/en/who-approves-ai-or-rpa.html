Who approves, AI or RPA?

Robotic process automation and AI agents are often compared as if one must replace the other. For an office that answers to clients and regulators, the more important question is who approves each action.

Oct 28, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • RPA repeats fixed steps well and breaks when the screen or the format changes.
  • AI copes with unstructured input, but its output varies and needs review.
  • Either way, a person should approve actions that matter before they run.

What each one does well

RPA records a sequence of clicks and keystrokes and replays it. It is predictable, and that is its strength. It is also brittle: a new field on a form or a different invoice layout can stop it. AI models read emails, documents and chats that do not follow a template. That flexibility comes with a cost: the same input can produce slightly different output, and the model can be wrong with confidence.

The approval question

In both cases the risk sits at the moment of action: a payment, a message to a client, a record changed in the system of record. A script that runs without review repeats its mistakes at scale. A model that acts without review adds new ones. The EU AI Act's article on human oversight asks that people be able to decide not to use an output and to stop the system. That principle is useful far beyond high-risk systems.

A practical split

  • Let AI read and prepare: understand the request, find the client, draft the entry.
  • Let a person approve the action, with the source in view.
  • Let the approved action run as a defined, repeatable step, not as free text from a model.

How Flow does this

Flow reads the request, prepares each step and waits for Do It. An approved action runs as a defined process in the connected system, and every step is logged.
