AI on your own infrastructure

Running AI on your own infrastructure means the content of the work does not leave the office. It also means the office takes on the job of running a system well.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Local processing removes the trip to an external cloud, not the need for security.
  • Plan for hardware, network segmentation, updates and access control.
  • Agree in advance who maintains the system and who can reach it.

What changes when processing is local

In a cloud setup, the provider runs the servers and you rely on its controls. On your own infrastructure, the model and the processing run on hardware you control. Client data does not travel to an external provider for processing. That is a strong property, and it comes with work.

A short checklist

  • Hardware: a machine with enough memory and processing power for the workload, in a secured location.
  • Network: the server sits in its own network segment, with only the connections it needs. NIST SP 800-53 covers this under its system and communications protection controls.
  • Updates: the model, the software and the operating system all need patching. Agree a schedule.
  • Access: who can log in, with which rights, and how access is reviewed and removed.
  • Logs: the same audit records you would expect from a cloud service, kept inside the office.

Who does what

Decide who owns the server, who is called when it misbehaves, and how changes are approved. A small IT team or an outside provider can do it, as long as the roles are written down.

In Flow

Flow-Onprem runs on a dedicated server inside the office network, and data does not go to an external cloud. Flow-Edge runs on employees' own computers, and the data stays on each computer. In both, approvals, logs and Undo work as they do in Flow's cloud.
