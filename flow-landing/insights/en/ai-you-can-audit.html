AI you can audit

Sooner or later someone asks: why did this happen, who approved it, and what did the system see? An office can answer only if the records were designed before the question arrived.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Logs should connect the input, the proposal, the approval and the result.
  • Record who approved, when and in which system, not just that something ran.
  • Agree the log scope with your security team, including what not to store.

What the rules expect

The EU AI Act requires high-risk AI systems to allow the automatic recording of events over their lifetime, so their functioning can be traced (Article 12). NIST SP 800-53, widely used as a control catalogue, has a whole family of audit and accountability controls: what events to log, what each record must contain, how records are protected and reviewed. Neither tells you to log everything. Both tell you to log what lets you reconstruct events.

A useful audit record

  • What arrived: a reference to the email, message or document that started the work.
  • What the system proposed, in which version of the process.
  • Who approved, edited or rejected it, and when.
  • What was executed, in which system, and the outcome.
  • Whether it was later reversed, and by whom.

Logs are data too

An audit trail can itself hold sensitive data. A log that copies whole client emails creates a second place to protect. A better design stores references and the minimum detail needed, with access to the log restricted and changes to it prevented.
