AI in insurance claims, fairly

Claims handling is document-heavy and deadline-driven, which makes it a natural place for automation. It is also where fairness rules and policyholder trust are tested.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Automate intake, document collection and follow-up; keep decisions with adjusters.
  • Some regulators require insurers to test algorithms and data for unfair discrimination.
  • Record what the system prepared and who decided, for every claim.

Where automation helps

A claim arrives by email or a portal with photos, invoices and forms. Someone checks what is missing, opens the file, asks for documents and tracks deadlines. Much of that can be prepared automatically, with the adjuster approving each step.

Where the rules bite

Colorado's SB21-169 requires insurers to test their use of external consumer data, algorithms and predictive models to make sure they do not discriminate unfairly on the basis of a protected class, and to take corrective action when they do. The EU AI Act lists AI used for risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance as high-risk (Annex III). The direction is clear: automated influence on outcomes has to be tested and explained.

Keep the decision human

  • The adjuster sees the documents, the summary and what is missing.
  • Approving, denying or changing a payment is an explicit human action.
  • The record shows what was prepared, what was changed and who decided.
