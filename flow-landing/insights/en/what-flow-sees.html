How Flow works

What Flow sees: email, WhatsApp, CRM and calendar

Flow works where your team already works. It reads the channels you connect, understands what needs to happen and prepares the action. Here is what it reads, what it does with it, and where the limits are.

Nov 13, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Flow reads only the channels and systems the office connects.
  • It reads in order to prepare actions, and every action waits for approval.
  • Incoming text is treated as data to analyze, never as an instruction.

The channels

  • Email: requests, documents and attachments.
  • WhatsApp: messages from clients and colleagues, where the office uses it for work.
  • CRM and other systems of record: who the client is, what is open, what was done.
  • Calendar: availability, deadlines and meetings.

Reading is not acting

Flow combines these sources to understand a request: who asked, what they need and what already exists. It then prepares one concrete action, such as a record, a reply or a calendar entry, and shows what it relied on. Writing happens only after approval.

Access is set by the office

The office decides which mailboxes, chats and systems are connected, and with which permissions. The rule of thumb is least privilege: Flow gets the access a process needs and no more. It is also one of the mitigations OWASP recommends for applications built on language models.

Why approval matters here

Messages from outside can contain text written to steer an AI system, a risk OWASP calls indirect prompt injection. Flow treats content from email and chats as data to analyze, not as instructions to follow, and because every action needs approval, a manipulated message cannot write to your systems on its own.
