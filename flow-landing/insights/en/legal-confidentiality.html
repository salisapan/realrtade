Legal automation without breaking confidentiality

Most of a law firm's administrative work can be automated. The duty of confidentiality to clients does not change because a tool is new. The design has to start from that duty.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Lawyers remain responsible for understanding the tools they use.
  • Client information should reach a tool only with the protection the duty requires.
  • Legal judgment stays with lawyers; automation handles the work around it.

What the profession says

In July 2024 the American Bar Association issued Formal Opinion 512 on generative AI tools. It addresses competence, confidentiality, communication with clients, supervision and fees. The core message is familiar: a lawyer must understand the capabilities and risks of a tool well enough to use it responsibly, and must protect client information when using it. Bar rules differ by country and state, and the same questions appear in each.

Where the risk sits

  • Client names, case details and documents sent to an outside service.
  • Drafts sent to a client or the other side without a lawyer's review.
  • Tools that learn from inputs, so one client's material could shape output for another.

A safe division of work

Let automation handle intake forms, filing documents in the right matter, deadlines in the calendar and routine follow-up. Keep advice, strategy and anything sent in the firm's name with a lawyer. GDPR Article 22 points the same way for decisions about people: significant decisions should not rest on automated processing alone.
