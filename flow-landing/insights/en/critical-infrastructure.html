AI in critical infrastructure

Energy and utility companies run two kinds of systems: the office systems that handle work, and the operational systems that control physical equipment. AI automation belongs on the first side.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Operational technology has safety and reliability needs that office IT does not.
  • Mandatory reliability standards govern cyber security for the bulk power system.
  • Keep AI workflows on business systems, segmented from control networks.

Two different worlds

NIST SP 800-82 describes operational technology as programmable systems and devices that interact with the physical environment, and explains why securing them must account for their performance, reliability and safety requirements. CISA treats industrial control systems as a distinct area of security for the same reason.

Mandatory standards

In North America, NERC's reliability standards, including the Critical Infrastructure Protection standards, set mandatory cyber security requirements for the bulk power system. Any tool that could touch the systems in scope needs to fit them.

Where AI fits

  • Work orders, supplier correspondence, permits and reporting on the business network.
  • Not control commands, setpoints or anything that changes physical equipment.
  • A clear network boundary between the two, with no path from the workflow tool into the control network.

Data that should stay in-house

Infrastructure documents, network diagrams and incident reports are sensitive even on the business side. Processing them inside the company's own network is often the right default.
