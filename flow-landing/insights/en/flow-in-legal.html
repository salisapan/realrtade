Flow in legal: requests, contracts and follow-up

Legal work is full of deadlines hidden in emails and documents. Flow reads them, records the deadline, files the document in the matter and prepares the next step. The lawyer approves each one.

Nov 13, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Flow reads court documents, contracts and client emails, and prepares the next step in the matter.
  • Deadlines go into the calendar with the source attached.
  • Confidential material is masked or stays inside the firm, depending on the setup you choose.

The work around the legal work

A decision arrives by email with a response period buried in the text. A client sends back a signed agreement. Opposing counsel asks to move a meeting. Each needs a record in the matter, a date in the calendar and often a short reply.

What Flow does

  • Reads the document, finds the deadline and prepares a calendar entry with a reminder.
  • Files documents in the right matter in your document system.
  • Drafts a short update to the client or the team.
  • Spots work that was done but never recorded as time, from emails and the calendar, and proposes time entries for the lawyer to approve.

Professional duties come first

Bar guidance on AI starts from duties lawyers already have. The American Bar Association's Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) points to competence, including understanding the benefits and risks of the technology, to the confidentiality of client information, to communication with the client and to reasonable fees. Approval by the lawyer before every action, and a full record of what was done, support those duties. They do not replace professional judgment.

Confidentiality by design

Privileged material should not travel further than it must. Flow's cloud masks sensitive fields before processing. Firms that want nothing to leave can choose Flow-Edge, on the lawyer's own computer, or Flow-Onprem, on a server inside the firm.

Sources

  1. American Bar Association, Formal Opinion 512 on generative AI tools (July 29, 2024)
