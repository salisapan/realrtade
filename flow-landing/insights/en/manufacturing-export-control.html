AI in manufacturing and export control

A manufacturer's documents often carry more than commercial value. Drawings, specifications and process data can be controlled technology, and sharing them, even inside a software tool, can count as an export.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Export rules can apply to technical data, not only to physical goods.
  • Releasing controlled technology to a foreign person can be a deemed export.
  • Know where AI processing runs and who can reach the data.

Technology is controlled too

The US Export Administration Regulations control the export of many items, including technology and software. A drawing or a specification can fall under them just as a part can.

Deemed exports

The Bureau of Industry and Security defines a deemed export as the sharing or release of controlled technology or source code to a foreign person within the United States. Processing controlled data with a tool whose operators or support staff could access it raises the same question: who could see this, and from where?

Questions before automating

  • Which documents in this process may contain controlled technical data?
  • Where would the AI processing run, and in which country?
  • Who at the provider could access the content, for support or monitoring?
  • Is that recorded and reviewed?

Keep controlled data in place

For controlled data, the simplest answer is often to keep processing on infrastructure the company controls. Purchase orders and delivery schedules may be fine in a cloud service with masking; engineering documents may not be.
