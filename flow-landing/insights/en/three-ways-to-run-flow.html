Your data stays where you decide

Three ways to run Flow

Where data is processed is a decision, not a default. Flow runs in one of three setups, and the office chooses according to how sensitive its data is and what its policies allow.

Nov 13, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Flow's cloud with masking: sensitive data is masked before processing.
  • Flow-Edge: data stays on the employee's own computer.
  • Flow-Onprem: a dedicated server inside the office network.

Flow's cloud with masking

Flow's dedicated secure cloud. Before processing, sensitive fields such as names, ID numbers and account numbers are replaced with placeholders. The model works on the masked text, and the mapping back to the real values stays with you. It suits offices that want to start quickly with strong protection.

Flow-Edge

Processing runs on the employee's own computer, and the data does not leave it. It suits work whose content should not leave the device at all.

Flow-Onprem

A dedicated server inside the office network. Data does not go to an external cloud. It suits organizations whose policy requires processing to stay in the building, and that have, or want, the infrastructure for it.

How to choose

  • What kind of data does the process touch: names and dates, or privileged and health information?
  • What do your policies, clients and contracts require about where data is processed?
  • Who will operate the setup, and on which hardware?

Approvals, the log and Undo work the same in all three. The setup changes where processing happens, not who is in control. Which setup fits your office is something we work out together on a call.

Privacy guidance points the same way

Israel's Privacy Protection Authority, in its guide on privacy-enhancing technologies in AI systems (December 2025), describes approaches such as removing or masking identifiers and processing data locally. GDPR Article 25 calls for data protection by design, including pseudonymisation and data minimisation. The three setups are practical ways to apply those principles.

Sources

  1. Israel Privacy Protection Authority, Guide on Implementing Privacy-Enhancing Technologies in AI Systems (December 2025)
  2. GDPR, Article 25: Data protection by design and by default
