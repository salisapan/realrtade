Zero retention: how to verify it

Many AI vendors say they keep nothing. The claim can be true, partly true, or true only for one component. It is worth checking, with any vendor, including us.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Ask what exactly is not retained: prompts, outputs, files, logs, backups.
  • Ask whether your data is excluded from model training, in writing.
  • Deletion should be verifiable, including on backups and storage media.

What the claim should cover

Zero retention usually means the content sent for processing is not stored after the response is returned. That leaves several questions open:

  • Are prompts and outputs written to application logs or monitoring tools?
  • Are files kept in a cache or a temporary store, and for how long?
  • Does a sub-processor, such as a model provider, keep anything on its side?
  • Is any of it used to train or improve models?

The places data hides

Logs and backups are the usual gaps. A service can process without storing and still copy content into debug logs or nightly backups. GDPR's storage limitation principle in Article 5 says personal data should be kept no longer than necessary for its purpose, which applies to those copies as much as to the main database.

Deletion you can check

When data must be deleted, it should be deleted from the media too. NIST SP 800-88 describes how storage should be sanitized so data cannot be recovered. Ask how a vendor handles this for disks it retires.

Questions to ask

  • Which components store what, and for how long?
  • Which sub-processors are involved, and what do they retain?
  • Is training on your data excluded by contract?
  • Can you receive this in writing, as part of the agreement?
