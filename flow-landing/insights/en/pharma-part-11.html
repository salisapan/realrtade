AI in pharma: GxP and Part 11

In regulated life sciences work, an electronic record has to be as trustworthy as paper. Any AI that prepares or changes such records inherits that requirement.

Aug 31, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Part 11 requires secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails.
  • Record changes must not hide what was recorded before.
  • Validation and human sign-off belong in the design, not after it.

What Part 11 asks

21 CFR 11.10 lists the controls for closed systems. Among them: validation of systems, the ability to produce accurate copies of records, limiting access to authorized individuals, and secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails that record operator entries and actions that create, modify or delete electronic records. Changes must not obscure what was recorded before.

How FDA reads it

FDA's guidance on Part 11 scope and application explains a narrower reading of which records fall under Part 11, and that records must still be maintained in line with the underlying predicate rules. It leaves no doubt that audit trails, validation and record retention remain central.

Applied to AI-assisted work

  • The AI prepares; a qualified person reviews and signs.
  • Every entry, edit and approval is time-stamped and attributed.
  • The process version is recorded, so a change in the workflow is itself traceable.
  • A change to a record keeps the earlier value visible.

Validation does not end at launch

A validated process stays validated only while it is monitored. Edits and rejections by reviewers are a useful signal that something has changed.
