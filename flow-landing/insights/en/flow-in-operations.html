Flow in operations: orders, suppliers and tasks

Operations lives between systems: an order in an email, a delivery date in a supplier's reply, a task in someone's calendar. Flow connects them and keeps the work moving, with a person approving each action.

Nov 13, 2026·3 min read

In short

  • Flow turns requests in email into orders, tasks and reminders in the systems you already use.
  • It follows up on open items, so nothing sits forgotten in an inbox.
  • Every action waits for approval and can be undone.

The work between the systems

An order confirmation arrives, a supplier moves a delivery date, a colleague asks to book a room or reorder supplies. None of it is hard. It is scattered, and the cost is the item nobody followed up on.

What Flow does

  • Reads orders and supplier messages and prepares the matching record or update.
  • Creates tasks and calendar reminders with the source attached.
  • Notices when a promised date has passed without an answer, and drafts a short follow-up.
  • Saves documents in the right place, such as the shared suppliers folder.

Approval stays simple

Each prepared action shows what Flow read, what it checked and what it will do. One Do It approves it. The office decides which actions need approval and who gives it, and every step is written down: what was done, who approved it, in which tool and when.

Start with the follow-ups

Follow-ups make a good first process. The rules are clear, the risk is low and the effect is easy to see: fewer items waiting without an answer.
